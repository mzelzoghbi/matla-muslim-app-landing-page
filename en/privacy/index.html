Arkan privacy policy

Last updated: 18 October 2026

Arkan (أركان) is a prayer-times, Quran and athkar app for iPhone, Apple Watch and Android, made by Mohamed Zakaria El-Zoghbi (ZaPlatform). This page explains what happens to your data, on both platforms.

In short

  • No account, no ads. We ask for no email or phone number, and the app shows no ads.
  • No tracking across apps. We don't use the advertising identifier (IDFA on iPhone, the advertising ID on Android), and nothing we collect is linked to other apps or websites. We don't sell any data.
  • Anonymous usage and crash data. We use Google's Firebase to learn when the app crashes, where it's slow and which features are used, so we can improve it. It carries no name or location, and you can turn it off in Settings.
  • Your location stays on your device. It's used to calculate prayer times and the Qibla, and is never sent to us or to Google.
  • We run no server of our own. Everything you save in the app (city, settings, bookmarks, highlights, your own athkar, downloads) is stored on your device only.

Location

  • Arkan asks for approximate location (on iPhone by default, on Android only). That's enough for prayer times and the Qibla.
  • Location is read when you open the app, to notice if you've moved to another city. It is not read in the background.
  • Everything is calculated on the device. Your location is not sent to us or to anyone else.
  • You can decline and pick your city by hand; the app works fully.
  • The Apple Watch may use its own location for the Qibla page, in the same way, on the watch only.

Usage and crash data (Firebase)

Arkan uses Firebase services, provided by Google as a data processor on our behalf:

  • Crashlytics (crashes and diagnostics): when the app crashes, a report with the crash log, device model, and the system (iOS or Android) and app versions.
  • Analytics (usage): anonymous events, such as which screens are opened and which features are used. Never your location, your city or what you write in «أذكاري».
  • Performance (iPhone only): measurements such as app start time and network load times.
  • Remote Config: the app fetches a few settings from Firebase (for example, which sections to show), so we can adjust them without an update. It sends only an installation identifier.

This data carries an app-instance identifier set by Firebase, which changes if you delete the app. We don't use the advertising identifier, we don't ask for tracking permission, the data is not used for advertising, and it isn't linked to your name or email, which we never have. Your location is not sent with it. As with any internet connection, Google sees your device's IP address.

You can turn off usage and crash data in Settings › الخصوصية › «مشاركة بيانات الاستخدام والأعطال»; sending stops and the app works as before. Remote Config still fetches its settings, but it collects nothing about how you use the app.

Google processes this data under Google's privacy policy and the Firebase privacy terms.

Notifications and the alarm

The adhan and reminders are local notifications scheduled on your device; none of them go through a server. On iPhone, the Fajr alarm is an iOS system alarm (AlarmKit).

On Android, the app asks for these permissions, each for one job:

  • Notifications, for the adhan and reminders.
  • Alarms & reminders (exact alarms), so the adhan and the Fajr alarm come on the minute.
  • Full-screen alarm, so the Fajr alarm you turn on can show over the lock screen, like a clock alarm. Nothing else uses it.
  • Media playback in the background, so the adhan, the Fajr alarm sound, recitations and the radio keep playing when you leave the app or lock the screen, with a notification to stop them.
  • Run at startup, to schedule the adhan again after the phone restarts.

The wake-up task (optional)

If you choose a task to turn off the Fajr alarm:

  • Microphone and speech recognition (iPhone), to say the waking dhikr. The app asks for on-device recognition when your device supports it. Audio is not stored or sent to us. On devices without on-device recognition, Apple processes the speech under its own privacy policy.
  • Motion, to count your steps (on Android, through the motion sensor, with no permission). Stays on the device.
  • Camera (iPhone), to scan a code you print and keep by the sink. Images are not stored or sent.

What the app connects to

Support and purchases

"Thank Arkan" purchases are optional and unlock nothing. They are processed entirely by the store you installed from: Apple through the App Store, or Google through Google Play Billing, each under its own privacy policy. We never see payment details or who you are. The app keeps only a note on your device that you supported it, to show you our thanks.

Apple Watch sync

The app sends your settings (city, method, athkar progress) from your iPhone to your watch directly, through Apple's system. It doesn't pass through any server of ours.

Backups

Your phone's own backup (iCloud, or your Google account on Android) may include the app's settings, so they come back on a new phone. That backup belongs to your account, under Apple's or Google's policy; we can't see it.

Children

Arkan is suitable for all ages. From anyone, of any age, it collects only the anonymous usage and crash data described above.

Deleting your data

What you save in the app is on your device: Settings → Reset the app erases it, and deleting the app erases all of it (on Android, so does clearing the app's storage). Usage and crash data isn't linked to who you are, so we can't pick yours out; turn sharing off in Settings and nothing more is sent.

Changes

If this policy changes, we'll update it here and change the "Last updated" date.
